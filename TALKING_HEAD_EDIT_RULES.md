# Talking Head Edit Rules

Standing instructions for editing my talking head videos in DaVinci Resolve.
Include this file with every raw video. Apply these rules unless I say otherwise in the job.

## 1. What you will receive

- The raw video file (one continuous recording with multiple takes, or one clip per take).
- The script as plain text, with sections numbered.
- Optionally: a timecoded transcript from Resolve, or an exported marker list.

If a transcript or marker list is included, use it as the source of truth over your own transcription.

## 2. How I mark takes while recording

Listen for these spoken cues in the audio. They will appear in the transcript.

| Cue | Meaning |
|---|---|
| "Section 3, take 2" | Slate. Spoken before a take starts. Groups takes by script section. |
| "Keeper" or "That one" | Spoken right after a take. This is the take to use. Overrides everything else. |
| "Again" | Spoken right after a take. Discard it. |
| "Pickup" | The next take covers only part of a section. Splice it into the keeper for that section. |

I leave about two seconds of silence before and after every take. Cut in that silence.

## 3. Take selection rules, in order

1. A take tagged "Keeper" or "That one" always wins for its section.
2. If no take in a section is tagged, use the **last complete take** of that section.
3. A take tagged "Again" is never used, even if it is the last one.
4. If the last take is incomplete (cut off, restarted mid sentence) and nothing is tagged, use the last complete take before it.
5. If no take in a section is complete, use the best partial takes and splice them. Flag the section in your notes.

"Complete" means the take covers the whole section as written in the script, start to finish, with no restart.

## 4. What counts as "best" when you have to judge

Only apply these when the rules in section 3 leave a tie. In this order:

1. Matches the script. Fewest deviations from the written line.
2. No filler. No "um", "uh", "so", "like" at the start, or long pauses.
3. No restart, stumble, or self correction inside the take.
4. Strongest energy. Pick the take where the delivery sounds most confident and natural.
5. Cleanest audio. No background noise, clipping, or room sounds.

You cannot see delivery or eye line from the transcript. When a tie comes down to delivery, pick the last take and flag it in your notes so I can check.

## 5. Assembly rules

- Keep sections in script order, regardless of recording order.
- Cut in the silence before and after each take. Do not cut inside a sentence.
- Remove the slate ("Section 3, take 2") and the tag ("Keeper", "Again") from the final cut.
- Remove dead air longer than about one second between sections unless the script marks a pause.
- Do not remove natural pauses inside a sentence.
- Do not add music, titles, B roll, or effects unless the job asks for them.

## 6. What to send back

- The timeline or project file.
- A short notes list with: each section number, which take was used, and why.
- A flag list of anything I should review: ties decided on delivery, spliced sections, sections with no complete take, and any line that drifts from the script.

## 7. If something is missing

- No script: build sections from the slates. If there are no slates either, treat each natural restart as a new take of the same section and apply the "last complete take" rule.
- No slates and no script: stop and ask me for the section breakdown before cutting.
